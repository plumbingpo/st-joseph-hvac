When Your System Fails in Columbia

If your system quits, how quickly help shows up matters more than anything else. That's why we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Urgent calls typically see a technician within a day, and we prioritize no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that keeps tripping, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Nearly all of these trace back to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You get the price before any work starts, with no surprises on the invoice.

A little caution before we arrive can head off a much bigger repair. An iced-over AC should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

How Columbia Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Most mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.
